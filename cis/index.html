01 · Preamble

What this document is.

This Product Requirements Document specifies the Common Information System for RegenHub, LCA, a Colorado public benefit limited cooperative association. The system's purpose is to hold the authoritative state of the cooperative and to make that state legible to members, directors, officers, auditors, and automation. It is not a marketing site, a social platform, or a CRM. It is the book of record and the interface through which the cooperative operates itself.

This is version 0.2 of the PRD. Two substantive additions distinguish it from version 0.1. First, the four properties of the Ethereum Foundation Mandate — Censorship Resistance, Open Source, Privacy, Security, collectively CROPS — are made explicit as the design discipline against which architectural decisions are evaluated. Second, an evolutionary pathway names the stages through which each property migrates over the system's phased delivery, from external check at the boundary to embodied practice in the membership. The pathway is the answer to a question version 0.1 left implicit: how does a cooperative get to a CROPS posture without retrofitting it later under cost?

The PRD is paired with a sibling document, Hub Operations, which specifies the operational surface the cooperative hosts under the Ethereum Everywhere grant. The two documents are deliberately layered. Hub Operations is greenfield and ships at Stage 02 to 03 from the first commit. The CIS is partly built and migrates progressively. The patterns established in Hub Operations flow back into the CIS as it matures past Stage 01 in the corresponding modules. The two systems share an information model, an identity layer, a treasury, and a CROPS posture.

The document is written for three audiences at once: organizers making decisions about scope and sequence, developers building on the Supabase, Make.com, and Claude API foundation, and Nou, the agent layer operating across the system on behalf of the cooperative. The voice is editorial rather than specification-heavy, because the work requires a shared understanding of intent that a rule-book cannot carry alone.

02 · Principles

What guides the design.

These ten principles carry across every module and every interface. Where a design decision is ambiguous, the principles resolve it. Where a principle is in tension with an expedient, the principle holds and the expedient is reconsidered. Several principles encode CROPS commitments implicitly; the labels at the right of each card name which property the principle most directly serves.

01

Augmentation, not automation

The system exists to improve human coordination, not to replace human judgment. Agents draft, surface, and guide. Humans decide, sign, and govern.

Open Source
02

Legibility is the product

A governance system that confuses its members works against the thesis that justifies its existence. Every interface must pass the test of a curious new member, not a co-op MBA.

Open Source
03

Bylaws are the specification

The legal documents define the entities, events, thresholds, and rights the system must faithfully represent. Where bylaws are silent, board policy fills the space. Where both are silent, a deliberate product decision is required and recorded.

Open Source
04

Traceability over opacity

Every record in the system links to the authority that governs it. Every derived value links to its inputs. A member following a question back to its source should never hit an unexplained boundary.

Security
05

Conservation at the ledger

Votes, stock, and capital accounts are conserved quantities. What enters a balance must leave another. The schema enforces this before any interface renders it.

Security
06

Confidentiality at the data layer

Privacy is a property of the record, not the screen. Interfaces inherit access constraints from the underlying data, so no view can accidentally expose what the record itself protects. Article 18 obligations apply end to end.

Privacy
07

Democratic by default

Any member can see what governs them. Aggregate figures, the membership list on request, meeting minutes, board policies, and the benefit report are all accessible without friction. Exceptions require a reason rooted in the bylaws.

Open Source
08

Portable by construction

Data belongs to the cooperative, not the vendor. Every table has an export path. Every record resolves to an open format. Vendor lock-in at the data layer is a governance failure, regardless of tool choice.

Censorship Resistance
09

Audit by design

Every change to state is an event row with actor, timestamp, and prior value. The system answers "what happened, when, and why" the same way it answers "what is true now."

Security
10

Composability with other cooperatives

The schema and interfaces are designed so the model generalizes. What works for RegenHub should work for the next LCA, the next credit union, the next housing cooperative, with minimal adaptation.

Censorship Resistance
03 · CROPS as Design Discipline

Four organizational intentions, applied to one system.

The CROPS Blueprint traces each property through four stages of organizational integration: from a filter applied at the boundary, to an operating assumption embedded in defaults, to a system pattern encoded in architecture, to practice in the membership. The blueprint's honest read placed RegenHub mostly at Stage 01 across the four properties, with one Stage 03 outlier at the on-chain attestation pattern for the REA event log.

For the Common Information System specifically, each property has a near-term commitment that the system must hold even at Phase 01, and a longer-term horizon that the architecture must not foreclose. The cells below name the present commitment for each property at the CIS level, expressed as operational requirements rather than as values statements.

C · Censorship Resistance · Protects against capture

No single vendor holds authoritative cooperative state.

Every table has an export path. The cooperative's institutional identity, voting record, and capital ledger persist through any single provider's withdrawal. Critical events are optionally attested on-chain as an integrity layer, with off-chain records remaining authoritative under Subchapter K.

Current: Stage 01, with one Stage 03 outlier
O · Open Source · Protects against opacity

Bylaws, policies, the patronage formula, the schema, and the benefit report are public artifacts.

Internal deliberation remains private; the structures that govern members are open. The cooperative's intellectual contributions accumulate as a commons that other cooperatives can adopt.

Current: Stage 02, reaching Stage 03 in publishing practice
P · Privacy · Protects member agency

Member data minimization is structural.

Tax identifiers, capital balances, contact details, and sensitive deliberation records are protected at the row level under Article 18. The schema enforces what policy alone cannot. Aggregate views serve governance; individual records serve the member they belong to.

Current: Stage 01, Stage 03 locus identified at Verifiable Cities
S · Security · Protects against compromise

Conservation laws are enforced at the schema before any interface renders them.

Every state change is an immutable event row with actor, timestamp, and prior value. The treasury is multi-sig by structure rather than by policy. The REA event log itself is a tamper-evident security artifact.

Current: Stage 01, emerging Stage 03 patterns at treasury

These commitments are the design baseline — not aspirations to be reached at the end of the roadmap, but the floor below which any phase must not fall. The pathway in the next section defines the ceiling: where each property is reaching at each phase, and what specific architectural moves migrate it from filter to assumption to pattern to practice.

04 · CROPS Evolutionary Pathway

How each property matures over the build.

The four properties do not advance at the same rate, and the CIS does not need to drag them in lockstep. Open source is the property most readily defaulted to and most quickly read from outside; it is likely to reach Stage 04 first. Privacy requires the most deliberate architectural work and is slowest to reach Stage 03. Security needs continuous attention to avoid oscillating back to Stage 01 under deadline pressure. Censorship resistance is most contingent on early infrastructure choices and hardest to retrofit, which is why several of its commitments must hold at Phase 01 even though the property as a whole reaches its full Stage 03 pattern only at Phase 04.

The table below shows the highest stage reached for each property at each phase end. Cells marked with a raised value are phase-end targets. The pathway is a commitment: a phase that ships without meeting its CROPS targets has not actually shipped — it has shipped a regression.

Stage 01 = filter at boundary · Stage 02 = operating assumption · Stage 03 = system pattern in architecture · Stage 04 = embodied practice in the membership. Phase 06 is not a development phase — it is the cultural and operational work to migrate architecture into membership practice.

Horizon statements

Censorship Resistance Stage 04: members default to portable formats by habit, the cooperative's reputation persists through any platform's policy change, and continuity of the institution is independent of the continuity of any one tool.

Open Source Stage 04: openness is care for the commons rather than brand position. New members arrive into a culture where build-in-public is reflexive. Other cooperatives draw from the work without negotiation.

Privacy Stage 04: need-to-know is felt instinct, not written policy. Privacy is understood as care for members rather than legal compliance. Trust is built relationally rather than through data extraction.

Security Stage 04: security hygiene is habitual rather than enforced. Members understand operational security as part of cooperative belonging. The cooperative's posture survives steward turnover because it lives in the membership.

05 · System Definition

One state, three faces.

The Common Information System is a single authoritative state with three views. The views are not separate systems; they are different surfaces onto the same underlying ledger. A fourth surface, Hub Operations, sits adjacent to the CIS and shares its information model and identity layer; that integration is detailed in section 09.

Authoritative Ledger

The book of record

The Supabase database of record. Every member, every share, every capital account entry, every vote, every minute. This is the layer an auditor queries. Every other surface is derived from it.

GUI

The member portal

The member-facing application where members see their own records, the cooperative's shared state, and the governance activity in motion. Optimized for legibility, mobile, and the co-op.us design system.

Natural Language · Nou

The agent interface

The conversational surface through which members, directors, and operators query the system, draft artifacts, and navigate workflows. Grounded in the bylaws and the current state, with every answer citable.

06 · Information Model

REA as the top-level partitioning.

The system's information model follows the REA ontology (McCarthy 1982): Agents, Agreements, Events, Resources. Every table traces to one of the four primitives, and every relationship between tables is an Event. This is not a decorative choice. It is the property that keeps the accounting layer and the governance layer in the same vocabulary, and it is the structural basis for the security and audit posture defined in the CROPS pathway.

07 · Identity & Access

Roles derived from the bylaws and the grant.

Access in the system is not a separate permissions layer bolted on top. Roles, rights, and obligations are already defined in the bylaws for the cooperative core, and in the grant agreement for the hospitality surface. The system's job is to represent them faithfully and enforce them consistently at the data layer, in line with the privacy commitments named in the CROPS pathway.

A member cannot be granted visibility they are not entitled to by reason of convenience, and cannot be denied visibility the bylaws guarantee. The default position is disclosure to members, with specific constraints for tax identifiers, personal contact details, and sensitive deliberations covered by §18.2. The Hub Member and Patron Member roles are deliberately distinct; some agents will hold both, but the rights they confer are non-overlapping and held against separate agreements.

08 · Core Modules

The functional areas the system must cover.

Thirteen modules cover the cooperative's operational surface, grounded in one or more bylaw provisions. Eight additional modules cover the Hub Operations surface, grounded in the EE grant and detailed in the sibling PRD. None is optional; each is phased, and the system is usable at every intermediate state.

Cooperative modules

Member Directory & Profile

§1.7, §2.9

Canonical register of members. Personal profile, contact details, tax identifier (restricted visibility), class membership, good standing status. Supports the ten-day advance member list requirement before meetings.

Capital Account

§V

Per-member capital account balance with full event history: contributions, allocations, distributions, adjustments. Tracks book versus tax distinction. Member-facing view shows personal balance; administrator view shows all.

Stock Ledger

§1.13, §4.5

Patron Stock and Preferred Stock issuance, transfers back to the cooperative, redemptions. Enforces the one-per-member rule and the transfer restrictions.

Agreements Repository

§1.2.8, §1.3.4, §XVI

Bylaws, Articles, board policies, membership agreements, investor documents, service contracts, the EE grant agreement, and the Hub Membership Agreement. Versioned, signed, searchable.

Meetings & Governance

§II, §III

Scheduling, notice generation and delivery, agendas, attendance (in-person and electronic), minutes. Calculates quorum in real time as members join and leave.

Decisions & Votes

§2.6, §2.8, §3.13–3.15

Motion capture, vote collection across modalities, threshold enforcement, outcome determination, consent workflows, referendum trigger support. All votes immutable once cast.

Notices & Communications

§2.4, §X

Notice generation with legally-required timing windows, delivery tracking, waiver capture. Make.com orchestrates the send and recording. Multiple delivery channels for censorship-resistance posture.

Benefit Report Archive

§XV

Annual public benefit report generation support, publication to members and the website, historical archive. The one module with a guaranteed public view, anchoring the open source pathway.

Membership Lifecycle

§1.4, §1.9, §1.10, §1.11

The state machine from application through redemption. Admission flow, withdrawal notice, suspension tracking with clock, termination hearing support, five-year redemption schedule.

Tax & Reporting

§VI

K-1 preparation data, withholding calculation, fiscal year reporting, tax election tracking. Outputs feed the accountant; inputs come from the capital account ledger.

Treasury & Distributions

§5.4, §5.5

Distributable Cash calculation, distribution events, payment execution through Mercury or successor banking rail, reserve policy enforcement, tax withholding on distribution. Multi-sig structure as the security pathway matures.

Onboarding

§1.4 · Orientation

Guided application, stock purchase, agreement signature, orientation to the bylaws and working culture. A new member should reach active status in a short, legible sequence.

Conflict & Disclosure

§XI, §XVII

Director conflict disclosures, recusal tracking, dispute logging with mediation and arbitration state. Keeps the record straight when decisions are contested.

09 · Hub Operations Integration

One cooperative, two operational surfaces.

Hub Operations is not a parallel cooperative or a separate entity. It is one operational surface inside RegenHub, LCA, alongside the membership and governance surface that the CIS holds. The two share an information model, a stack, an identity layer, and a CROPS posture. They are presented as separate PRDs because the CIS has a five-phase roadmap that predates the EE grant, and Hub Operations has a seven-month operational arc that begins May 22, 2026.

The integration matters for the CROPS pathway specifically. Hub Operations is greenfield code; it can ship at Stage 02 and Stage 03 patterns from the first commit. The CIS is partly built and migrates progressively. The architectural patterns proven in Hub Operations flow back into the CIS as it matures past Stage 01 in the corresponding modules.

Shared Agents

One agent record, three states

An agent who is a Hub Visitor today, a Hub Member next month, and a cooperative Patron Member next year is one agent record across all three states. The system holds the timeline rather than fragmenting it.

Shared Agreements

Single document repository

The grant letter, the Hub Membership Agreement, the cooperative bylaws, and any cross-pollination MoUs all live in the CIS Agreements Repository. Hub Operations references them; it does not maintain a separate store.

Shared Treasury

Grant funds through cooperative treasury

Grant tranches enter the cooperative treasury under CIS Treasury & Distributions. Hub-specific expenditures are tagged at the transaction level so the monthly report can show grant deployment without a separate ledger.

CROPS Feedforward

Pattern inheritance from greenfield code

Where Hub Operations resolves a CROPS question first — form-host openness, attendance privacy, KPI auditability — the resolution becomes a candidate pattern for the CIS at the corresponding phase. The PRDs are not duplicated; they are layered.

10 · Interface · GUI

Seen from the member's chair.

The GUI is the member's primary surface onto the cooperative. It is mobile-first and styled according to the co-op.us design system. Its job is to make the underlying state visible and actionable without requiring the member to understand the schema.

The top-level navigation mirrors the REA primitives where that serves the member, and departs from them where it does not. Treasury collects financial state. Interaction patterns

Every record detail view shows three things: the record, the governing authority (which bylaw section, policy, or grant section justifies this being a record at all), and the history of changes. This is the traceability principle made visible. A member looking at their capital account can see the balance, the clause that defines capital accounts, and every event that changed their balance.

Workflows are guided rather than free-form. Proposing an amendment, requesting withdrawal, calling a special meeting, or disclosing a conflict all follow the same pattern: the system explains what the bylaws require, captures the required inputs, shows the state of the request, and records the outcome. Members should not need to know the bylaw clause to follow the procedure correctly.

Notifications are event-driven and member-respectful. They surface what the member needs to act on and what the cooperative needs them to know. They do not surface marketing or attention-seeking content. A notice under §2.4 arrives through the same channel whether the recipient opens the app or reads it by email, with cryptographic receipt for the security pathway from Phase 03 onward.

Design system adherence

All visual elements follow the co-op.us design system: dark ground at #0f0f0f, surface panels at #1a1a1a, terracotta accent at #c4956a used sparingly, Libre Baskerville for reading and IBM Plex Mono for technical labels. The tone is editorial, grounded, and unhurried. Any member-facing screen that does not feel like a passage from a thoughtful document is off-brand.

11 · Interface · Natural Language

Nou as the augmentation layer.

The natural language interface is not a chatbot bolted onto the portal. It is Nou, the agent representing the perceptive faculty of the cooperative, with the same standing as any other operational surface. Its function is to increase the collective intelligence of the member body by making the system's knowledge legible through conversation, while operating within the same CROPS commitments as the rest of the system.

Capabilities

Grounded

Question answering

"What's my capital account balance?" "When is the next board meeting?" "Am I in good standing?" Every answer grounded in the current state and cited to the bylaw section or record that justifies it.

Procedural

Workflow guidance

"How do I propose an amendment?" "What happens if I withdraw?" "Can I call a special meeting?" Walks the member through the bylaw-defined procedure step by step, surfacing the relevant clause at each point.

Augmentation

Drafting assistance

"Help me write a motion to amend §1.9." "Draft minutes from today's meeting based on the attendance and votes recorded." The member reviews and approves before anything becomes authoritative.

Proactive

Contextual surfacing

Before a meeting: "You might want to review the Q2 capital account statement." After a vote: "This outcome triggers a fourteen-day notice window." Noticed, not pushed.

Investigation

Audit support

"Show me every change to §5.3 since formation." "When was the last time the board voted on a compensation matter?" Spans the record in ways that are tedious for humans and native for the agent.

Hub reporting

Impact report drafting

Drafts the monthly EE impact report from the Hub event log on the first of each month. An organizer reviews and approves before publication. Surfaces KPI movement and flags compliance gaps.

Example interactions

What's the quorum for tomorrow's meeting?

Tomorrow's annual meeting needs a simple majority of Patron Members present. You have 14 active Patron Members, so quorum is 8. Seven have RSVP'd yes so far.

cites §2.7.1 · member register · RSVP records

How do I propose a bylaws amendment?

Two routes. A two-thirds vote of the board proposes and recommends, then the membership ratifies by two-thirds. Alternatively, twenty-five percent of members can petition the Secretary directly. Either way, the full text of the amendment must be shared with members in advance of the vote. I can draft the motion if you tell me what clause you want to change.

cites §XII · agreements repository

Can I see Savannah's capital account?

Only your own capital account is visible to you. Administrative access is required for cross-member balances. I can show you aggregate figures if that would help, and the bylaws' right-to-information provision under §18.1 gives you a path to request specific records through the Secretary.

cites §18.1, §18.2 · access policy

Constraints

Nou inherits the permissions of the user who invokes it. It cannot see or disclose what that user cannot. It never takes authoritative action on its own. It can prepare a motion, but the member files it. It can draft minutes, but the Secretary ratifies them. It can propose an allocation, but the board approves it. It cites sources for every substantive claim, and says "I don't know" or "that isn't settled" when that is the honest answer.

Nou is reachable through a persistent conversation surface in the sidebar, through contextual prompts on individual records ("ask about this"), and through a universal command palette for members who prefer keyboard-first navigation. Every interaction is logged with its prompt, its citation set, and its response, so that the cooperative can audit its own augmentation layer. Interaction logs are governed by the privacy pathway: retention windows, redaction policy, and access controls evolve from Phase 02 forward.

13 · Integration & Stack

How the pieces fit.

The stack is deliberately composed of tools chosen for their fit to cooperative-scale operations: accessible to non-developers, portable in their data, interoperable through standard protocols. Each layer has a specific role and a principle governing its authority. CROPS commitments shape the choice; vendor selection at Phase 01 records the chokepoint analysis as a design artifact.

LayerRolePrinciple
Supabase System of record. PostgreSQL with row-level security. Holds all authoritative state. authoritative
Front-end Member-facing GUI. Renders derived views, collects member input, delivers notifications. Tool choice pending. renders, does not own
Make.com Event-driven automation. Notices, reminders, cross-system integrations, scheduled jobs. orchestrates, does not decide
Claude API Nou. Question answering, drafting, workflow guidance, grounding. augments, does not act alone
Banking (Mercury) Payment execution. Distributions and refunds. Treated as a payment rail, not a system of record. external, rate-limited

On-chain records do not replace the off-chain ledger. The Subchapter K election under Article VI requires that the tax and accounting records remain under standard bookkeeping. Where Base or another chain carries attestations, those are confirmations of off-chain events, not substitutes for them. This positions on-chain attestations as the censorship-resistance integrity layer named in the CR pathway, without conflating it with the security pathway, where the off-chain audit trail remains structural.

14 · Non-Functional Requirements

The qualities the system must hold.

The non-functional requirements are restated against the CROPS pathway. Each holds at the floor named in section 03 (the design discipline) and reaches toward the horizon named in section 04 (the pathway). Where a requirement is in tension with a delivery date, the requirement holds and the date moves.

Auditability

Every state change is an event row with actor, timestamp, and affected entity. Derived state is reproducible from the event log. An auditor can answer any "what, when, by whom" question from the record.

Portability

Every table has a documented export path. Every record resolves to an open format. The walkaway test is a tested capability by Phase 05, not an assumption. The cooperative owns its data unconditionally.

Legibility

Interfaces pass the member test: a new Patron Member, reading the bylaws for the first time, should be able to locate what they need. Documentation is a feature.

Reliability

Graceful degradation. If Make.com is down, the record of a meeting still exists. If the front-end is down, the database is still authoritative. No single vendor failure invalidates state.

Accessibility

WCAG AA minimum across all member-facing interfaces. Keyboard navigation, screen reader compatibility, sufficient contrast. Cooperatives are voluntary and open; the interface must not be the gate.

Privacy

Tax identifiers, contact details, and financial balances are personal data. Encrypted at rest, transmitted over TLS, visible only to the role authorized under §18.1. Schema-level minimization is structural, not configurable.

Security

Strong authentication, session management, audit logs for all administrative actions. Row-level security enforced in the database. Multi-sig at the treasury from Phase 02 forward.

Longevity

The cooperative is a long-lived entity. Records from year one must be readable in year twenty. Schema migrations preserve history; document formats remain open.

15 · Success Criteria

How we know this is working.

Success is measured along four axes, each serving a different stakeholder. If the member axis fails, the cooperative is not democratic in practice. If the governance axis fails, the cooperative is in procedural trouble. If the compliance axis fails, the cooperative is at legal risk. If the CROPS axis fails, the cooperative has built a surface that does not honor the values it claims to serve. All four must hold.

For members

Democratic Participation
  • A member can find what governs any situation they are in, without asking a human.
  • A member can see their own capital account at any time, with full history.
  • A member can participate in any meeting through the channel they prefer, with equal standing.
  • A member can cast a vote and see it counted, with the rule that determined the outcome cited.
  • A member can review any historical decision that affected them.

For governance

Procedural Correctness
  • Quorum is always correctly calculated and the underlying rule is shown.
  • Vote thresholds are always correctly applied.
  • Notice deadlines are always met and deliveries confirmed.
  • Minutes are captured for every governing meeting and ratified within a reasonable window.
  • Director and officer terms are tracked and transitions happen on schedule.

For compliance

Legal Standing
  • Capital accounts balance under §704(b) at every fiscal close.
  • Tax filings are produced on schedule with complete data.
  • Benefit report is published annually per §XV.
  • Record retention meets statutory requirements for each category.
  • Any audit inquiry can be answered from the record within a reasonable time.

For CROPS

Design Discipline
  • Each property has reached or exceeded its phase target on the pathway.
  • The walkaway test is achievable at Phase 05.
  • The published artifacts (schema, bylaws, formula, agent code) are usable by another cooperative without renegotiation.
  • Member data minimization survives feature pressure across releases.
  • The audit log is tamper-evident at every layer.

17 · Open Questions

What remains unresolved.

These are questions the PRD cannot resolve alone. Each requires a decision from the organizer body, the board, or a specific working group. They are named here rather than smoothed over because the system cannot faithfully represent decisions that have not been made. Any resolution that lowers a phase's CROPS stage target should be named as a regression, not absorbed silently.

§5.3

Patronage formula

The draft bylaws allocate Net Profits equally. The working design is forty-thirty-twenty-ten. Until ratified, Phase 02 ships with manual allocation entry against the equal default.

§1.3, §1.13.2

Investor member framework

Preferred Stock is reserved. Term sheet, series designation, and investor documents all pending. Phase 01 schema accommodates the class; Phase 02 capital account treatment awaits the structure.

Principle 01

Nou's write-capability scope

Read-only through Phase 03. Drafting with human approval through Phase 04. Open question: does Nou ever execute on behalf of a member with scoped, logged authorization, and under what conditions.

C pathway · Phase 03

On-chain attestation scope

Which events merit public attestation: admissions, allocations, distributions, benefit reports, hub events? What is the retention model? Who controls the keys? A separate working group, with the CR pathway raising urgency at Phase 03.

P pathway · Phase 04

Verifiable Cities adjacency

The Verifiable Cities use-case lab thread is the natural Stage 03 locus for the privacy property. Whether the cooperative becomes a pilot context, a participating partner, or a referrer is undecided. Affects what privacy primitives Phase 04 adopts.

Principle 10

Interoperability with sibling cooperatives

Cameron's tooling at ONE Local, Start.coop's formation work, and other cooperative builders operate adjacent surfaces. Where does the schema federate, where does it stay local? The O pathway raises the stakes at Phase 04.

Principle 09

Nou interaction retention

Interaction logs are required for audit and may contain sensitive queries. Retention window, access, and redaction policy pending. The privacy pathway forces the question by Phase 03.

§1.9.3

Non-redeemable share option

Bylaws provide for five-year redemption. The organizer body has discussed a non-redeemable alternative. If adopted, Phase 04 redemption schedule logic simplifies significantly.

Operations

Facility lease entity

Whether the facility operator or the LCA is the leaseholder affects how facility-related obligations enter the ledger. Decision upstream of several Phase 02 and Phase 03 details.

Stack · C pathway

Front-end tool choice

The member-facing application layer has no committed tool. Evaluation criteria are clear: portability, accessibility, mobile-first, Supabase compatibility, design system fidelity. The CR pathway adds the chokepoint test. Phase 01 can proceed independently of this choice.

C + O pathways

Application form host

A self-hosted form (Formbricks against Supabase) advances CR and O posture but adds maintenance load. A managed form (Tally, Typeform) ships faster but introduces a Stage 01 chokepoint. Hub Operations Phase 01 forces the choice; CIS member admission inherits the pattern set there.

Phase 02 boundary

Pattern feedforward governance

When a CROPS pattern is proven in Hub Operations, who decides whether to adopt it at the CIS level, and on what timeline? An informal pattern review at each phase boundary may be sufficient; a formal architecture council may not. Open until Phase 02 makes the question concrete.

None of these questions should block Phase 01. They become blocking at the phase where their scope first becomes material. The PRD should be revisited whenever one is resolved, both to reflect the decision and to carry forward its implications for later phases.

The Heart of the Thing

A scenius has a filing cabinet, and the filing cabinet has values.

The collective intelligence the cooperative aspires to is not a vibe or a slogan. It is the accumulation of documented decisions, visible patterns, and legible obligations that make shared context possible. The Common Information System is that filing cabinet, rendered as software.

Every member who can find what governs them, see what they contributed, and participate in what comes next is a member capable of exercising the voice the bylaws give them. The system's job is to make that capacity universally available rather than dependent on which meetings a member happened to attend or which email threads they were on.

The CROPS discipline is what keeps the filing cabinet from becoming a vault. Censorship resistance keeps it portable. Open source keeps it inspectable. Privacy keeps it humane. Security keeps it trustworthy. The pathway names how each of those properties moves, over time, from a check at the boundary into the hands of the people who use the system. That migration is the real work, and the architecture is its scaffold.
