Cooperative Identity

Why We Publish This

The International Co-operative Alliance defines cooperatives by seven principles. This public treasury statement is a direct expression of three of them.

ICA Principle 3
Member Economic Participation
Members contribute equitably to, and democratically control, the capital of their cooperative. Publishing capital account structure makes this democratic control visible and legible — to members, prospective members, and the public.
ICA Principle 5
Education, Training, and Information
Cooperatives inform the general public about the nature and benefits of cooperation. Open accounting demonstrates through live data that a cooperative can operate transparently without sacrificing member privacy.
ICA Principle 7
Concern for Community
Cooperatives work for the sustainable development of their communities. Financial transparency builds the trust that makes community-rooted institutions viable. We treat our balance sheet as a public record, not a private asset.
Our open accounting commitment
  • Aggregate financial position published continuously from live sources
  • Revenue and expense categories disclosed without individual member identification
  • Capital account structure and surplus allocation method disclosed publicly
  • Material notes and contingencies disclosed as they arise
  • Full opening financial statements available to members and the board of directors
