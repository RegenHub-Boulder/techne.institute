Articulation · May 2026

Techne Cooperative

An articulation, for members and close parties.

A proposal for member considerationBoulder, Colorado2026

In February we filed a Colorado Limited Cooperative Association under the name RegenHub, LCA. Six months later, the thing that LCA holds has continued to grow — and the name no longer quite contains it. What follows is an articulation of where we are now, what's coming clear, and what we're calling it.

This is not a pivot. The cooperative we filed is the cooperative we are. The public benefit purpose — cultivating scenius — is the public benefit purpose we still hold. What might change is that the name catches up to the shape.

What follows is a proposal, not a decision. Whether to rename, how membership and substrate take their final form — these are choices for the cooperative membership to make together. This articulation is an offer of language for that conversation.

A note on scope: this proposal focuses on landing the cooperative. Related questions about charitable and educational work belong in a separate conversation that follows.

In brief

  • Proposal: rename the cooperative to Techne Cooperative, with RegenHub continuing as a DBA — the name of the headquarters at 1515 Walnut.
  • Multiple bodies of work nest inside Techne — communities of practice, ventures, programs — each with its own relationship to the cooperative.
  • Federated governance: Techne Coop Members govern cooperative-scale matters; each community of practice (RegenHub, LVB, etc.) governs its own internal affairs. Memberships layer.
  • Communities of practice (Learn Vibe Build, Neighborhood Village Project, Open Civics) function as pathways into membership. Partner events (Boulder Startup Week, Cohere Boulder) serve as convergence points — distinct from membership pathways.
  • A shared information substrate — directory, treasury, inventory, governance — would align the bodies of work without homogenizing them.
  • The flywheel: scene-building cultivates ventures; ventures voluntarily commit equity to the cooperative (give-first lineage); the basket compounds; capital flows back to the scene, to new ventures, and to members. Scene-builders share in what they help create.
  • Starting point: RegenHub members are the seeds of this new chapter; 1515 Walnut remains the headquarters. Membership broadens outward over time, at the pace deep trust allows.
  • The legal entity, the public benefit purpose, the eight organizers, the lease, the patronage architecture — all preserved regardless of how the proposal lands.
· Ten sections
01 What's becoming clear The cooperative grew beyond the name we filed.

When we incorporated, RegenHub was the cooperative and the co-working space and the loose collection of ventures forming around both. One name for three things. That was honest at the time — the cooperative was RegenHub, because RegenHub was the only thing the cooperative had yet held.

That is no longer accurate. The cooperative now holds:

  • The co-working community at 1515 Walnut — the founding body of work, the place, the room where this began.
  • An educational current — Learn Vibe Build, the pedagogy that has formed around AI tools and right use of them.
  • A set of ventures and inquiries — Parachute, Postage, Habitat, and others — building tools and infrastructure that ventures elsewhere can also use.
  • An expanding constellation of communities — Neighborhood Village Project, Open Civics, the Coordination Games — each pursuing right relationship with technology in their own domain.

The cooperative is the underlying body that holds all of this. RegenHub is one of the things it holds — specifically, the co-working community of practice in Boulder. We're keeping the name because it has earned it, because the people who showed up at 1515 Walnut showed up under that name, and because the place itself wants to be called what it is.

The cooperative needs a name that reflects the whole.

02 The name Techne Cooperative is what we are. RegenHub is where we meet.

Techne — τέχνη — is the Greek word for productive knowledge: the craft of bringing something into being, where art and skill and practical wisdom were not yet separated.

It names what the cooperative is actually about: the recovery of craft in an age that has tried to separate the maker from the making, the tool from the wisdom of its use, the community from the technologies that mediate it.

If the proposal advances, RegenHub would continue as a doing-business-as name. The legal entity would remain the same, the SOS filing carries forward, the public benefit purpose preserved. RegenHub would become the name of the headquarters: the co-working space at 1515 Walnut, second floor, where the cooperative gathers and where its first community of practice still meets.

Two names doing two jobs.

03 What Techne is, in one sentence The inquiry into right relationship between tools and the people who use them.

Techne the word holds an inquiry — right relationship between tools and the people who use them. Techne Cooperative is the people doing that inquiry together: the cooperative of those building Boulder's tech, civic, and community scene.

The inquiry

The same question — what does it mean to use this tool well, in service of life rather than at its expense? — runs through every body of work the cooperative holds. AI in Learn Vibe Build. Civic infrastructure in Open Civics. Mutual obligation in Neighborhood Village Project. Co-working in RegenHub. Computing-as-soft-landing in Parachute. The tools differ. The question is the same.

The mission

Cultivating scenius — the collective intelligence that emerges from sustained proximity of culturally aligned people. In practice, this means cultivating Boulder's scene: the tech meetups, the civic projects, the community gatherings, the spaces where people come together and the work that holds them together. The cooperative is made of the people doing this scene-building work, and exists to make sure they benefit from what grows from it.

The cooperative

The cooperative is the holder. Not because the cooperative owns every body of work — it does not — but because the cooperative is the membership container through which people stand in relationship to the inquiry and to one another. To be a member of Techne is to be standing in this question with others.

Bodies of work

Bodies of work are the operable units. Some are communities of practice. Some are ventures. Some are programs. Some are inquiries that haven't yet decided what they are. The cooperative provides the conditions — legal, economic, infrastructural, cultural — under which bodies of work can form, grow, and recognize one another.

The shared substrate

What aligns the bodies of work without homogenizing them. A common pattern of directory, treasury, inventory, and governance — offered to each, adopted on its own terms.

The place

Boulder, Colorado, at 1515 Walnut Street, where the Great Plains meet the Rocky Mountains. The cooperative is bioregional. The communities and ventures it holds reach further, but the cooperative itself is rooted here.

04 Bodies of work Communities of practice, ventures, and programs nest inside Techne — each with its own relationship to the cooperative.

Not every body of work nested inside Techne has the same relationship to the cooperative. Two patterns are clear today; others may emerge as the cooperative grows.

Pattern 1

Held and incubated

Ventures and programs the cooperative holds equity or patronage interest in — incubated by the cooperative, vested in by it, but not necessarily owned outright. Surplus flows back through the cooperative's patronage system. Members who built the body of work are recognized in proportion to what they contributed.

Examples · Learn Vibe Build · parachute.computer · co-emerging ventures from the membership

Pattern 2

Aligned peers

Independent organizations with shared inquiry. They are not owned by the cooperative — they are in relationship with it. They share substrate where it makes sense to share. Their members can become Techne members through bridge programs, and Techne members can participate in their work.

Examples · Neighborhood Village Project · Open Civics

These patterns are not a hierarchy. They are different kinds of alignment, each appropriate for different bodies of work. A body of work can also move between patterns over time — what begins as an aligned peer relationship may, with shared work, become something more deeply integrated. What begins inside the cooperative may spin out into independence.

As the cooperative grows, additional relationship patterns may take shape — for example, charitably-sponsored bodies of work that need a nonprofit vessel. Those are real questions, but they belong in a separate conversation that will follow this one.

05 Membership pathways One cooperative membership. Each community governs its own. Bridge programs as pathways.

Federated, not centralized

Membership lives at two layers. Techne Cooperative Member is the membership at the cooperative level — described by the existing bylaws (Cooperative Member, Co-working Member, Community Participant, Investor Member). The cooperative governs cooperative-scale matters: shared infrastructure, the lease, patronage architecture, the public benefit purpose.

Each community of practice within Techne — RegenHub at 1515 Walnut, Learn Vibe Build, and others as they form — has its own internal member governance over its own affairs. RegenHub members make RegenHub decisions; LVB cohort members shape LVB programming; aligned peers like NVP and Open Civics are already self-governing at their own level.

A single person can be a member at multiple layers — a Techne Cooperative Member, a RegenHub member, a LVB cohort alum — each with its own scope. The layers nest; they don't replace each other.

Bridge programs — the pathway in

Bridge programs are communities of practice. Places where people return, develop, contribute, and form relationships over time. Sustained participation is the key: events bring people into proximity once; bridge programs hold people in inquiry over months and years.

Through sustained participation, people develop the standing relationship that can lead to deeper engagement with the cooperative — Class 3 by default, Class 2 if they want a desk, eventually Class 1 by invitation when the relationship is deep enough.

Examples:

  • Learn Vibe Build — a cohort-based AI learning practice with an alumni community, incubated within the cooperative.
  • Neighborhood Village Project — an aligned-peer practice of place; a community of practice around neighborhood-scale civic recovery.
  • Open Civics — an aligned-peer network of civic innovators with its own bi-weekly assemblies and grant rounds.

A bridge program does not need to be held by the cooperative. What it needs is shared character: an inquiry into right relationship with tools, a community of practice forming around it, alignment with the cooperative's purpose.

Partner events — convergence points

Distinct from bridge programs. Partner events are gatherings where the cooperative meets and works alongside aligned communities. They are convergence points, not pathways — moments where new relationships can form, but not in themselves a sustained practice.

Examples:

  • Boulder Startup Week — the city's annual convergence of founders, builders, and supporters; a partnership Techne participates in and may grow involvement with.
  • Cohere Boulder — another Boulder gathering Techne helps shape.

The distinction matters. A bridge program is something you can belong to. A partner event is something you can show up at. Both serve the cooperative — they do different work.

Coordination Games is currently taking shape as a venture; it may grow a community-of-practice layer that becomes a bridge program in time. The boundary between shapes is permeable.

06 The substrate A common information system. Four primitives. Same pattern, different bodies of work.

Across every body of work, a common information system holds the practical layer:

Directory

Members, roles, and the labor records that feed patronage.

Treasury

Real-time visibility into shared accounts and the flows between them.

Inventory

Resources, instruments, and the bodies of work each community keeps in circulation.

Governance

Operating agreements, member commitments, and the proposals that move them.

Each body of work uses these primitives on its own terms. Together they make the cooperative legible to itself — and they let work, value, and recognition flow between bodies of work without losing their distinctness.

Why these four?

They are the four faculties through which any organized body perceives itself: who is here (directory), what we hold (inventory), what is flowing, and where (treasury), under what agreements (governance). Less than four and the cooperative cannot see itself clearly. What a cooperative cannot see, it cannot recognize, coordinate, or distribute fairly.

In a patronage-based cooperative this becomes concrete. To distribute surplus by contribution, the cooperative has to know who contributed (directory + labor records), what surplus flowed from where (treasury), which bodies of work were involved (inventory), and under what agreements distribution happens (governance). All four, integrated. Less than four and patronage becomes approximation — or wishful thinking.

In a cooperative holding heterogeneous bodies of work — some owned, some aligned peers, others as new patterns emerge — the four primitives become a lingua franca. Each body of work runs its own directory, treasury, inventory, and governance on its own terms. Because the pattern is shared, work and value and recognition can flow between bodies of work without each having to reinvent the operating layer.

This is what makes "the cooperative as an intelligence" an operating claim rather than a metaphor. Intelligence requires sensory apparatus. The four primitives are the apparatus. Cultivating scenius, in practical terms, is the long work of giving the scene a way to see itself — so that contribution can be recognized, surplus can be distributed honestly, and the accumulated context that makes a scene generative can be held in common rather than lost to memory.

This substrate is what is actively being built. It is one of the central reasons the cooperative exists: to develop the tools that aligned communities and cooperatives everywhere will need, by using them in our own work first. Parachute is the venture most directly oriented around this work.

07 How this grows Scene-building cultivates ventures. Ventures give equity to the cooperative. Capital flows back to the scene, to new ventures, and to members.

If the cooperative is the basket, the flywheel is what fills it — and what keeps the work of filling it sustainable.

Scene-building cultivates ventures

The work of building the scene — running meetups, hosting gatherings, stewarding communities of practice, organizing events, holding space for breathwork and yoga and Tai Chi, supporting newcomers, maintaining the Boulder tech and civic fabric — creates the conditions in which new ventures emerge. Without this work, the scene goes thin and ventures stop forming. With it, ventures emerge naturally.

Ventures give equity, in the give-first lineage

When ventures form within the cooperative, they voluntarily commit equity back to it — a typical pattern is around 10% — out of the same "give first" principle that has shaped Boulder's tech culture through TechStars. This is not a tax or a fee. It is mutual recognition: the venture acknowledges that it grew from the scene; the cooperative is vested in its success. The give is structural, not transactional.

The cooperative as basket

The cooperative holds equity stakes across the ventures it has incubated. As ventures grow, the cooperative's basket compounds in value. Imagine a venture giving 10% at a $5M valuation: a $500K stake. If that venture grows to $100M, the stake is worth $10M. Multiplied across many ventures across many years, the basket can become substantial — without ever extracting from any single venture. (Worked figures here are illustration, not promise; actual venture trajectories vary.)

Capital flows back, three ways

The basket does not sit in an account. The cooperative deploys it, with member governance, in three primary streams:

  • Recognition — patronage to the people building the scene. Meetup organizers, breathwork leaders, gardeners, civic stewards, infrastructure-builders. The work that creates the conditions for everything else gets named, valued, and rewarded.
  • New ventures — the cooperative as venture studio, incubator, accelerator, and school all in one. Resourcing the next generation of ventures emerging from the scene. School to train, studio to incubate, accelerator to grow, basket to hold.
  • Member benefit — dividends to members and governance over allocation. Members both shape how the basket grows and benefit from its growth.

And running through all three: shared infrastructure — substrate, space, tooling — that benefits every body of work the cooperative holds.

The cycle

Stronger scene → more ventures emerge → bigger basket → more capital → stronger scene. Each turn of the cycle increases the cooperative's capacity to support what comes next. Most scenes lose this loop — value leaks out to distant platforms, exits, or capital structures that have no relationship with the people who did the scene-building work. Techne's structural commitment is the loop closing locally: the scene-builders share in the upside of what their work makes possible.

Where we are starting

RegenHub is where this begins. The current RegenHub cooperative members are the seeds of this new chapter — the people whose work brought us here. 1515 Walnut continues to be the headquarters. Membership extends naturally outward over time: from the RegenHub core toward other scene-builders across Boulder's tech, civic, and community fabric. Not all at once — we move at the pace deep trust allows. The intent is broadening; the work of broadening is its own sustained practice.

A note on the worked example: figures used here (10% equity, $5M to $100M trajectories) are illustrative, not promises. Specific equity terms, governance over allocation, and distribution rules remain to be worked out by the cooperative membership.

08 A cybernetic inheritance Intelligence that embodies wisdom — not intelligence for its own sake.

A note on lineage, because it matters.

Techne does not pursue intelligence for its own sake — neither human intelligence concentrated in a few minds, nor machine intelligence concentrated in a few platforms. The inheritance we are claiming is older and stranger: a cybernetic, humanitarian tradition that asks intelligence to embody wisdom. Intelligence that helps us understand and apply our tools toward the protection of human life, of habitat, of the living planet that holds both.

Brian Eno's word scenius — collective intelligence emerging from sustained proximity of culturally aligned people — is the public benefit purpose we filed under. Todd has named the same thing in another register: the cooperative as an intelligence that embodies wisdom. These are the same claim. One speaks from a creative tradition; the other from a cybernetic one. Both describe what the cooperative is for.

09 What stays the same Most things. If adopted, the rename is the paper catching up to the practice.

Many things.

  • The legal entity, its filing, its public benefit designation.
  • The eight organizers who signed on in February.
  • The capital accounts, member agreements (in development), patronage architecture.
  • The lease at 1515 Walnut. The Friday meetings. The room.
  • The commitment to soil-not-enterprise: the cooperative provides the conditions; the bodies of work are not its to own.
  • The principle that members are recognized for what they contribute — labor, revenue, capital, community — not just for capital alone.
  • The discipline of the substrate test: does this regenerate or extract?

If the proposal advances: the articles would be amended to register the name; a DBA filing would preserve RegenHub; the bylaws would accommodate Techne membership and the heterogeneous-bodies-of-work pattern. None of these are pivots. They are the legal and operational steps that would bring the paper into alignment with the practice — if the membership chooses them.

10 Where this goes next What would unfold if the proposal advances.

Some of what comes next:

Rename Articles amendment, DBA filing, member-agreement updates.
Substrate Building directory · treasury · inventory · governance primitives the bodies of work can adopt. Parachute is positioned to do much of this work.
Bridges Naming the bridge programs we already have, articulating the membership pathway through them.
Public Updating techne.coop and the cooperative's other public surfaces to reflect what is now coming clear.

We will move at the pace the substrate test allows — strengthening relationships, skills, agency, and economic capacity for the people in the room before we expand the room.

If you are reading this, you are one of those people. We would like to know what lands, what doesn't, and what is missing.
