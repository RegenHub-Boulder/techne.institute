Proposed Revisions to Bylaws v.1 and Class A Patron Member Membership Agreement v.1 — Organizer Rationale for v.2 Drafts

Jeff — following our work on the initial formation documents, the organizers undertook a careful review of the Bylaws and Membership Agreement drafts against the Colorado Uniform Limited Cooperative Association Act (ULCAA), C.R.S. §§ 7-58-101 et seq., standard cooperative practice, and the Cooperative's specific operational context. This memo summarizes the revisions we've incorporated into the v.2 drafts and the thinking behind each.

We are sharing our reasoning so you can tell us where we've gotten it right and where our analysis needs correction. We are not presenting these as final positions, and we recognize we are not lawyers. Your counsel is essential at each step — we welcome your response on every point. The v.2 documents are published as annotated drafts at techne.institute/legal/bylaws-v2/ and techne.institute/legal/membership-agreement-v2/, with each change highlighted and the rationale surfaced inline. The serialized change index for each document is at the Bylaws changes page and the Membership Agreement changes page.

Part I Bylaws Revisions
BL-08 · Preamble, First WHEREAS · see change card
Statutory Citation Correction — PBCA to ULCAA § 7-58-104

The first WHEREAS clause cited the Public Benefit Corporation Act of Colorado (PBCA), C.R.S. Title 7, Article 101, Part 5, as the governing authority for RegenHub's public benefit purpose. The PBCA governs benefit corporations organized under Title 7, Article 101 — we question whether PBCA has jurisdiction over a Limited Cooperative Association organized under Title 7, Article 58. We believe RegenHub's public benefit authority is ULCAA § 7-58-104, the LCA-specific public benefit provision.

We have replaced the PBCA citation with a reference to ULCAA § 7-58-104 throughout the Preamble. This is not a change in intent — it is our effort to ensure the Cooperative's governing documents cite the statute we believe applies.

BL-01 · Preamble, Fourth WHEREAS · see change card
Document Hierarchy — Mandatory ULCAA Provisions Above Articles

The v.1 conflict resolution hierarchy placed the Articles of Organization above the ULCAA, and retained the PBCA as a final fallback. We believe Colorado law does not permit an organizational document to subordinate mandatory, non-waivable statutory provisions. Under C.R.S. § 7-58-111, certain ULCAA requirements appear to be non-waivable and may not be overridden by any organizational document — though we defer to your reading of the statute.

We have revised the hierarchy to: mandatory ULCAA provisions first, then Articles of Organization, then Bylaws, then remaining ULCAA provisions. The PBCA reference has been removed. This change does not restrict any right the Cooperative or its organizers wished to preserve — we believe it reflects the legally correct order, but welcome your correction.

BL-02 · § 1.8.1 · see change card
Suspension — Notice and Hearing Before Effect

The v.1 suspension provision permitted the Board to suspend a member immediately upon a majority vote, without prior notice or opportunity to respond — except where criminal conduct was alleged. We believe immediate-effect suspension procedures of this type may be vulnerable to challenge as inconsistent with the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and would welcome your view on the risk level here.

We have added: (1) written notice of the conduct at issue before suspension takes effect (except in cases of criminal conduct, where immediate suspension remains available); (2) a five-business-day window for the member to submit a written response; (3) a mandatory Board hearing within thirty days; and (4) a written determination within ten business days of the hearing. We believe these procedural steps protect members from arbitrary action and would, in our view, reduce the Cooperative's exposure in the event of a legal challenge to a suspension.

BL-03, BL-A02 · §§ 1.7.3, 1.8.2 · see change card
Equity Redemption Timeline — Five Years to Twelve Months

Both the voluntary withdrawal provision (§ 1.7.3) and the involuntary termination provision (§ 1.8.2) permitted the Board to delay redemption of a departing member's equity for up to five years. In our view, a five-year redemption window may be commercially unreasonable and risks constituting an impermissible restraint on the statutory dissociation right under C.R.S. § 7-58-1101 — we would welcome your assessment of that risk. Members who contributed capital at the outset of the Cooperative's formation would have no practical path to recovery of their investment within a reasonable time horizon.

We have shortened the maximum redemption period to twelve months, consistent with standard cooperative practice. The Board retains discretion over method (cash, promissory note, or combination) — only the maximum timeline is changed.

BL-A03 · § 2.2 · see change card
Quorum — Electronic Participants Count

The v.1 quorum provision required a quorum to be determined by physical presence, while § 2.1 authorized electronic meetings. Members participating electronically could not count toward quorum under the v.1 standard, which appeared to us to make valid virtual meetings structurally impossible for a Cooperative whose membership is partly distributed. We have aligned the quorum standard with the electronic meeting authorization, but welcome your read on whether this was a genuine conflict or something the v.1 language could support by interpretation.

BL-A04 · § 3.16 · see change card
Director Compensation — Guaranteed Payment Carveout

The v.1 prohibition on director compensation appeared to us to inadvertently bar IRC § 707(c) guaranteed payments — what we understand to be the standard mechanism under Subchapter K for compensating member-directors for services to a partnership-taxed entity like an LCA. Without this carveout, we believe the Cooperative has no clear path to compensate working organizers for their labor through the cooperative structure. We have added an express carveout consistent with §§ 707(a) and 707(c) of the Internal Revenue Code, and would welcome your confirmation that this achieves the intended effect.

BL-A05 · § 5.2 · see change card
Patronage Allocation — Board Cannot Amend Methodology Unilaterally

The v.1 profit allocation provision gave the Board unconstrained authority to change the allocation methodology at any time, without member approval. This appeared to us to allow silent changes to the core economic bargain of membership — the ratio between labor patronage, revenue patronage, and community contribution that determines each member's capital account. We have routed methodology changes through the Patronage Plan amendment process, which requires majority patron member approval — which we believe is consistent with democratic member control under the ULCAA.

BL-A06 · § 5.1 · see change card
Qualified Income Offset — IRC § 704(b) Compliance

We understand that Treasury Regulation § 1.704-1(b)(2)(ii)(d) requires a qualified income offset (QIO) provision for capital accounts to have substantial economic effect under IRC § 704(b). Without the QIO, the IRS may reallocate items of income and loss inconsistently with the Cooperative's patronage accounting structure, which we believe could undermine the Subchapter T benefits the Cooperative intends to use. We have added the QIO provision and a corresponding member acknowledgment of capital account maintenance standards — and would welcome your confirmation that this approach is technically sound.

BL-05 · § 12 · see change card
Confidentiality — Scope Narrowed to Genuinely Non-Public Information

The v.1 confidentiality clause covered "any and all information received by or through the Cooperative, pertaining to the records of the Cooperative, its Members, and the operations, activities or transactions of the same." We understand that courts may decline to enforce overbroad confidentiality clauses of this type, particularly when they capture publicly available information or members' own professional activities — though we would welcome your view on how enforceable the v.1 scope would be. The breadth also created a practical problem we were uncertain about: whether members could describe their own membership without risking breach.

We have narrowed the obligation to genuinely non-public information designated as confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given the circumstances. Standard carveouts have been added: public domain information, prior knowledge, independent development, and legally required disclosure. Members may describe the existence of their membership and the general nature of the Cooperative's activities in professional contexts.

BL-07 · Schedule A-1 through A-4 · see change card
Membership Schedule — Placeholder Entries

The membership schedule rows for each class contained only placeholder text. We have added structured placeholder rows identifying where share prices and dues will be formally populated by Board resolution. The share price and dues amounts remain subject to Board determination — the schedule structure provides the framework for that adoption.

Part II Membership Agreement Revisions
MA-01 · § 1 · see change card
Mission Statement — Cooperative Purpose, Not Facilities Description

The v.1 opening described RegenHub as "a coworking space and community venue in Boulder, Colorado, providing affordable workspace and event infrastructure." This framing omits the Cooperative's legal structure, public benefit purpose, and the multi-dimensional scope of its operations. For a document whose opening recital defines what the member is joining, we felt this description understated the nature of the relationship.

We have replaced it with language drawn from the Cooperative's Articles of Organization and public benefit filing: organized to cultivate collective intelligence and scenius through right relationship between tools and the people who use them, operating across physical, digital, and programmatic dimensions. This accurately describes what the member is becoming part of.

MA-06 · §§ 1, 1.1, 1.5 · see change card
Class Scope — Agreement Identified as Class A Patron Membership

The Agreement did not identify itself as governing Class A patron membership specifically. With a Techne co-op agreement anticipated and additional membership classes possible, this ambiguity creates a risk that future classes inadvertently fall under these terms. We have added a scope statement limiting the Agreement to Class A patron membership and signaling that additional classes require separate instruments.

We have also added a thirty-day advance notice requirement for dues increases to § 1.5. The v.1 provision gave the Board unconstrained discretion to change dues at any time without notice. In our view, an unannounced dues increase could effectively force members out. We believe a notice requirement is a baseline fairness protection consistent with the Board's good-faith duty to members under the ULCAA — but we would welcome your view on whether this constraint is advisable or creates complications.

MA-02, MA-03 · §§ 3.4.1–3.4.4 · see change card
IP Framework — Defined Terms and Member Ownership Protections

The v.1 IP section used work-for-hire and IP assignment language without defined terms, leaving the scope of any assignment ambiguous. In a cooperative whose members are practitioners and builders, we believe the risk of inadvertently capturing a member's independent professional work is material — and a deterrent to membership.

We have added definitions for "Cooperative-Commissioned Work" (the category subject to assignment: work that is authorized by an officer in writing, expressly designated as work-for-hire, and compensated beyond standard membership) and "Applicant IP" (the protected category: work predating the Agreement, work outside cooperative scope without cooperative resources, and independent professional practice). The assignment scope has been narrowed to Cooperative-Commissioned Work only. The moral rights waiver has been similarly narrowed. Portfolio display rights have been adjusted to a notice-and-silence-as-consent model rather than requiring affirmative permission.

MA-04 · §§ 2.1–2.3 · see change card
Patronage Plan — Statutory Grounding and Member Acknowledgment

The v.1 profit-sharing provision gave the Board discretion to set and change the allocation formula unilaterally. We have tied allocation to the Patronage Plan required by C.R.S. § 7-58-1004 and added a 90-day adoption deadline to prevent indefinite deferral of the Cooperative's core economic mechanism. A member acknowledgment of capital account maintenance standards has been added to address what we understand to be the Treasury Regulation § 1.704-1(b)(2)(ii)(b) documentation standard for substantial economic effect.

MA-05 · § 5.1(d)(e) · see change card
Termination Grounds — Articulable Facts and Materiality Requirements

The "detrimental conduct" and "misrepresentation" termination grounds in v.1 were broad enough to be applied pretextually. We have added two constraints: the Board's judgment must be based on specific, articulable facts (not general impressions), and the detriment or inaccuracy must be material to membership or patronage. A thirty-day cure period has been added for breaches capable of cure, before termination may take effect. We believe these changes produce a procedurally more defensible termination process and would reduce the risk of a successful challenge.

MA-07 · § 9.4 · see change card
Status of Parties — Positive Owner-Member Characterization

The v.1 § 9.4 defined the parties' relationship exclusively through negative disclaimers: Applicant is not an employee, not an independent contractor, not a partner. While accurate, the provision said nothing about what Applicant is. If a court or third party looks to the Agreement to characterize the relationship and finds only a list of what it is not, the most analogous established category becomes the interpretive default — which may not be what was intended.

We have added a positive opening statement: "Applicant is an owner-member of the Cooperative with the rights and obligations set forth in this Agreement and the Governing Documents." We believe the term "owner-member" is legally significant under the ULCAA — we understand it to signal equity ownership (membership stock) and democratic participation (voting rights), distinguishing the relationship from the categories the disclaimers negate. We would welcome your view on whether this characterization achieves the intended effect.

MA-A01, MA-A02 · §§ 4.1–4.3 · see change card
Voluntary Withdrawal — Explicit Provision Added

The v.1 Agreement had no voluntary withdrawal provision, despite C.R.S. § 7-58-1101 giving members a statutory dissociation right that we believe this Agreement cannot eliminate. Silence on this point appeared to us to create unnecessary ambiguity about the mechanics of departure and the economic consequences. We have added an explicit withdrawal provision with a ninety-day stock redemption timeline, treatment of accrued patronage credits, survival of key obligations (IP, confidentiality, indemnification), and a reference to the statutory limitations on distributions under C.R.S. § 7-58-1007.

We have also published the v.2 documents with inline annotations on every changed passage, showing the v.1 original text and the reasoning for each revision. These are accessible at the links above and are intended to make your review as efficient as possible — you can hover any highlighted change to see both what changed and why.

We anticipate the most discussion around items with substantive legal judgment involved: the redemption timeline change, the QIO provision, the IP framework architecture, and the patronage plan grounding. On the statutory citation issues (PBCA/ULCAA, hierarchy, quorum) we believe our v.2 positions are correct — but we recognize we may have misread something, and particularly welcome your view on each of these.

Please respond at your convenience. We are targeting final governing documents ahead of our August launch and pitch day, and would like to have your feedback incorporated before then.

Thank you for your work on the formation documents, and for your continued engagement with the Cooperative.
